Site induction guide

Construction site induction and QR sign-in.

Digital site access can connect induction, certification information and attendance instead of relying on a paper sign-in sheet alone.

Why site induction matters

A site induction gives workers and visitors information relevant to the site, its rules, hazards and arrangements. A digital process can make the acknowledgement and record-keeping side easier to manage.

The HSA publishes typical induction topics for construction contractors. The exact content should reflect the particular site and work being carried out.

What does a site induction typically cover?

Induction content is decided by the contractor for the particular site, but typical topics include the site rules, emergency and first-aid arrangements, welfare facilities, traffic management and pedestrian routes, PPE requirements, and how to report hazards, incidents and near misses. Site-specific hazards — overhead lines, exclusion zones, live work areas — belong in the induction too, and the content should be reviewed as the site changes.

Visitors and delivery drivers usually get a shorter briefing than site workers, but their attendance still needs to be recorded and the relevant rules communicated before access.

Paper sign-in sheets versus digital records

A paper sign-in sheet answers one question: who wrote their name this morning. It doesn't connect that name to induction status, certification expiry dates or previous attendance, and it is hard to search after the fact. A digital sign-in linked to worker records can answer the questions that actually come up — was this person inducted, is their Safe Pass in date, when were they last on site — at the time of access rather than during a later audit.

The record is also legible and time-stamped, which matters when attendance information is needed quickly during an emergency roll call or an investigation.

A simple digital workflow

Scan. A worker uses a site QR code to access the sign-in and induction process.

Check. The site process can collect the information needed before access is recorded.

Acknowledge. The worker confirms they have received the relevant induction information.

Record. Attendance is recorded digitally so site management has a clearer view of who is on site.

Review. The attendance record can be used alongside worker, certification and safety records.

Safe Pass and site records

SOLAS states that Safe Pass is a mandatory one-day health and safety awareness programme for construction workers in Ireland, subject to the applicable rules and recognised schemes. A site-management system can help a contractor keep a record of certification information, but it does not issue or replace a Safe Pass card.
