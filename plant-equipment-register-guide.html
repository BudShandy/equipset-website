Plant & equipment records

Plant and equipment register guide for Irish construction.

A practical approach to keeping machine records, inspections, certificates, faults and supporting documents organised across a construction fleet.

What is a plant and equipment register?

A plant and equipment register is a structured record of the machinery and equipment a business owns, operates or manages. It should make it easy to identify each item and find the information needed to manage it safely and efficiently.

Typical records can include the machine ID, make and model, serial number, location, inspection history, maintenance information, certificates, operator requirements and documents associated with the asset.

Why keep inspection records against the machine?

Inspection information is easier to manage when it stays connected to the asset rather than being scattered across paper forms, spreadsheets and folders. A digital record can show previous checks, failed items, corrective actions and supporting photographs in one place.

HSA guidance for construction plant gives examples of weekly machine inspections being documented on GA2 records and relevant GA1 thorough-examination certificates being kept on file for certain machinery. Requirements depend on the equipment and the work being carried out, so the applicable inspection and examination arrangements should be determined by the responsible competent persons.

GA1 reports and equipment records

The HSA GA1 template is used to record the thorough examination and testing of lifting equipment. The HSA states that the form must be completed by a competent person and is not itself an approved or statutory form; reports can be produced in other formats.

EquipSet can be used to store a resulting GA1 report with the relevant machine record and track its associated document information. It does not perform the examination or issue the GA1.

A practical digital workflow

01

Register the asset

Create one record for each machine, with identifying information and the documents relevant to it.

02

Label the machine

Use a QR code so the machine record can be opened quickly at the point of work.

03

Complete the check

Use the appropriate inspection checklist and record the result, notes, evidence and faults.

04

Follow actions

Turn failed checks or identified defects into visible actions and keep the history against the asset.

Keep the register useful

A register works best when it is kept current. Assign responsibility for updating records, review expiry information, remove or archive equipment that is no longer in use and make sure inspection checklists reflect the actual equipment and task.

Digital records can make information easier to retrieve, but they do not replace risk assessment, competent inspections, maintenance, training or other duties that apply to the equipment and work activity.
