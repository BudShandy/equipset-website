Irish construction compliance checklist.

A practical starting point for organising the records, checks and site controls that Irish construction teams need to manage. Use it as an internal review tool alongside your own risk assessments, procedures and competent-person advice.

Work through the checklist site by site.

Construction compliance is not a single document. It is a collection of people, site, plant, training, inspection and safety records that need to remain current and available. This checklist groups the main areas into a simple review structure.

1. Site & access

  • Site details, responsible persons and emergency arrangements are recorded.
  • Site induction process is defined and records are retained.
  • Site access and attendance can be checked when required.
  • Worker certification information is checked before relevant work or access.
  • Visitors and contractors are controlled under the site's arrangements.

2. Workers & certification

  • Worker identities and relevant roles are recorded.
  • Safe Pass status is recorded where applicable.
  • CSCS cards/training are checked for applicable safety-critical tasks.
  • Other required training, licences and competency records are current.
  • Expiry dates are reviewed rather than relying on paper files alone.

3. RAMS & work planning

  • Risk assessments and method statements are prepared before relevant work.
  • RAMS reflect the actual work, site conditions and controls.
  • Responsible persons and required controls are identified.
  • Workers receive relevant briefings and acknowledgement is recorded.
  • RAMS are reviewed when work, conditions or controls change.

4. Plant & equipment

  • A current plant and equipment register is maintained.
  • Routine inspections and checks are recorded against the equipment.
  • Defects are recorded and followed through to action.
  • Required examination, inspection and certification records are retained.
  • Relevant GA1 reports are available where applicable.

5. Site inspections

  • Planned site inspections are scheduled and completed.
  • Findings and photographic evidence are recorded where useful.
  • Corrective actions have an owner and target date.
  • Outstanding actions are reviewed rather than left in inspection forms.
  • Inspection history can be retrieved for the site.

6. Incidents & observations

  • Incidents, near misses and safety observations can be reported.
  • Immediate actions and follow-up actions are documented.
  • Lessons learned are communicated where appropriate.
  • Records are retained and can be reviewed for recurring issues.
  • Serious incidents are handled under the applicable reporting requirements.

7. Documents & evidence

  • Key site documents have a clear owner and current version.
  • Inspection, induction, briefing and certification records are organised.
  • Evidence can be retrieved without relying on one person's inbox or filing cabinet.
  • Records are protected and retained in line with company requirements.
  • Site managers know where to find the information they need.

8. Review & corrective action

  • Open compliance and safety actions are reviewed regularly.
  • Overdue actions are visible to the people responsible for closing them.
  • Expired certifications and documents are identified promptly.
  • Trends from inspections and incidents are reviewed.
  • The checklist itself is reviewed when the company's work or risk profile changes.
Important: This checklist is a practical management aid, not legal advice and not a substitute for the requirements applying to a particular project, employer, contractor or item of equipment. Certification, inspection and examination requirements should be confirmed against current Irish legislation and competent-person advice.
